## The canon transform and why it is lawful

Two same-machine process runs of a request are byte-identical apart from the timing leaves — probed during M15 slice 1 on this engine: 17-digit element sizes (3.8076468739412803), changes (-0.2807483011665701) and tracked values reproduced exactly across processes, while wall_ms and its derivates moved. The canon therefore drops exactly `wall_ms total_ms mesh_ms solve_ms thermal_ms cost_fit stop_reason fit_ms seed_ms seed_n` wherever they appear (`cost_fit` is entirely wall-derived; `stop_reason` prose embeds measured times; the three projection leaves are wall- or store-derived), and the budget case additionally drops `refusal`, whose sentence embeds the timed stop text. Remaining floats quantize to 6 significant digits, object keys serialize sorted (serde_json's BTreeMap map). Consequences: wall-time regressions visible ONLY in the timing leaves are out of the golden's sight (V17/V18 own walls); any change in the engine's numbers, labels, structure or field VALUES bites.

## Version-bump policy

Every envelope carries the engine identity `fieldsmith 0.2.0 / fenics-rs 0.1.0` (`store::engine_identity`). ## Named gaps

Cross-machine float bit-identity is NOT promised (GOAL section E): the 6-digit quantization bounds what another machine's last-ulp differences can move. Text leaves embedding change floats at `{:+.1}` precision carry a near-zero but nonzero cross-machine rounding-boundary risk. serde cannot apply `deny_unknown_fields` inside the internally tagged question enum — unknown keys INSIDE a question silently pass (schema.rs states the same). The engine-version drift in mesh DOF counts would break the golden together with the ship-tie semantic asserts — one recording, then one re-bless.
