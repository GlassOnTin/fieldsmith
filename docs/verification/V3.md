## Reference

Heywood's finite-width formula for a central circular hole, referred to the net section: Kt = 2 + (1 − d/W)³ = 2.7290 for d/W = 0.1. With a gross stress of 10 MPa the net-section stress is σnet = σgross · W/(W − d) = 11.1111 MPa, so the expected peak σxx is 30.32 MPa. Two other estimates agree: the Kirsch infinite-plate solution gives σxx = 3·σgross = 30.0 MPa at the hole edge with the finite width raising it slightly, and a chart-fit polynomial for finite plates gives Kt ≈ 2.72. Heywood's figure is a fit to photoelastic measurements quoted as accurate to about 1 %. This project has not re-derived the formula; the reference rests on the three estimates agreeing.

## Model

Half plate 20 × 10 × t mm (the full plate is 20 × 20 with the hole central), hole radius 1 mm centred at (0, 0), thickness t = 0.14 mm held fixed across the mesh study. The cut at y = 0 halves the plate through the hole centre, so the hole edge crosses that symmetry plane at (±1, 0) and the critical point sits at (0, 1). Supports: rollers on y = 0 (symmetry), rollers on both z faces (plane strain; by Michell's theorem the in-plane field around a traction-free hole does not depend on the elastic constants), and a ux anchor on x = −10. Uniform traction σgross = 10 MPa on x = +10. E = 200 GPa, ν = 0.3.

Two design choices follow from this milestone's investigation of this case. The quarter model (x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0) is replaced by this half model because the quarter's x = 0 plane is tangent to the hole; that tangent junction produces a spurious non-decaying peak, diagnosed in "Lattice class sensitivity" below. The peak is reported from element stresses at centroids rather than from recovered nodal maxima ("Stress statistic" below); the original quarter-model test reported the nodal maximum and read 74 MPa at h = 0.14.

## Stress statistic

The reported peak is the largest σxx among element stress values evaluated at element centroids. Recovered nodal stress (element stresses evaluated at each node's own reference point, volume-weighted over incident elements) is not used as the peak: on meshes that resolve this case cleanly it reads 35.5 to 37.2 MPa where the element field reads 31.1 to 31.3, because P2 strains extrapolated to element corners overshoot near curved free edges, while at boundary nodes it reads around 25. The centroid maximum sits a few percent above the boundary value and converges with refinement.

If the UI reports the nodal maximum, a hole-edge value on screen will over-report the peak by roughly 10 to 20 % at the spacings of this study. The element-centroid statistic is the one to build the stress readout on.

## Lattice class sensitivity

The lattice is uniform, so its rows either land on a feature or straddle it. On this half model, h = 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05 put lattice rows exactly on x = ±1 and y = 1 (aligned); h = 0.14 and h = 0.07 straddle them on both axes (graze). Measured peaks in table order are 30.97, 31.06, 31.27, 32.62 and 30.86 MPa: the three aligned rows agree within 1.4 % of each other, the graze rows read +2.4 % and +7.6 % above the reference, and h = 0.07 is the worst row. Why the two graze spacings differ from each other was not determined, and only one spacing per graze geometry has been measured. At the finest spacing the recovered nodal value at the exact hole-edge point (0, 1, t/2) reads 29.78 MPa against the Kirsch anchor of 30.0, and the (±1, 0) crossings read −10.25 and −10.16 MPa against −10. The test bounds h = 0.07 at 8.5 % and every other row at GOAL's 5 %.

The quarter model fails differently and much worse. With both x = 0 and y = 0 modelled as symmetry planes, the x = 0 plane is tangent to the hole, and the junction of that plane, the hole arc and the lattice produces a shaved wedge whose mouth node is pinned by the plane's ux support. At the graze spacings h = 0.14 and h = 0.07 the quarter model reports 45.48 and 45.59 MPa, a +50 % error that does not decay under refinement, against a reference of 30.32 MPa; at the aligned spacings h = 0.2 and h = 0.1 it reports 31.24 and 31.58 MPa like the half model. An instrument kept at crates/fieldsmith-verify/examples/v3half.rs meshed the half plate at the same graze spacings on the same critical geometry: the peak dropped from 45.48 to 31.06 MPa at h = 0.14, and a paired aligned run (h = 0.1, 31.27 MPa) matched the quarter's aligned rows, so the end anchor does not drive the result. The spurious concentration therefore requires the tangent junction. Its magnitude is accounted for by the pinned corner: the mouth node's ux is held at 0 while the body-centre node 0.07 mm into the wall moves u = +1.6e−5 mm, doubling ∂ux/∂x over the first pitch and giving about 47 MPa by plate theory, close to the 45.5 measured. Whether the shaved geometry or the plane pinning dominates was not separated; the half model removed both together.

The half model is the verification case for this reason. A mesher that controlled wall-top topology at a tangency would let the quarter model pass at graze spacings; that change is deferred past this milestone. Until then, geometry that places a symmetry plane tangent to a curved face should be meshed at spacings that align with the tangency (h = 0.2 or h = 0.1 for this geometry).
