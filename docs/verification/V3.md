## Reference

Heywood's finite-width formula for a central circular hole, referred to the net section: Kt = 2 + (1 − d/W)³ = 2.7290 for d/W = 0.1. With a gross stress of 10 MPa the net-section stress is σnet = σgross · W/(W − d) = 11.1111 MPa, so the expected peak σxx is 30.32 MPa. Two other estimates agree: the Kirsch infinite-plate solution gives σxx = 3·σgross = 30.0 MPa at the hole edge with the finite width raising it slightly, and a chart-fit polynomial for finite plates gives Kt ≈ 2.72. Heywood's figure is a fit to photoelastic measurements quoted as accurate to about 1 %. This project has not re-derived the formula; the reference rests on the three estimates agreeing.

## Model

Full plate 20 × 20 × t mm with a central ⌀ 2 mm hole (radius 1 mm at (0, 0)), thickness t = 0.14 mm held fixed across the mesh study. Two symmetry reductions are verified across the same five mesh spacings (h = 0.2, 0.14, 0.1, 0.07, 0.05 mm):

1. **Half plate** (20 × 10 × t mm, y ≥ 0): the symmetry cut at y = 0 halves the hole orthogonally at (±1, 0), leaving the critical point (0, 1) on an interior x-plane. Rollers on y = 0 and both z faces (plane strain), ux = 0 anchor on x = −10, uniform traction σgross = 10 MPa on x = +10.
2. **Quarter plate** (10 × 10 × t mm, x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0): symmetry rollers on both x = 0 and y = 0, plane-strain rollers on both z faces, and uniform traction σgross = 10 MPa on x = +10. Here the x = 0 symmetry plane is tangent to the hole boundary at the critical mouth point (0, 1). E = 200 GPa, ν = 0.3.

## Stress statistic

The reported peak is the largest σxx among element stress values evaluated at element centroids. Recovered nodal stress (element stresses evaluated at each node's own reference point, volume-weighted over incident elements) is not used as the peak: on the meshes of this study it reads 32.6 to 38.3 MPa where the element field reads 29.1 to 32.4 (8 to 20 % above the centroid value), because P2 strains extrapolated to element corners overshoot near curved free edges.

If the UI reports the nodal maximum, a hole-edge value on screen will over-report the peak by roughly 8 to 20 % at the spacings of this study. The element-centroid statistic is the one to build the stress readout on.

## Lattice class sensitivity and the M19 quarter-junction fix

The background BCC lattice is uniform, so its planes either land on a feature or straddle it. On both models, h = 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05 put lattice planes on x = ±1 and y = 1 (aligned); h = 0.14 and h = 0.07 straddle them on both axes (graze). On the half model, measured peaks in table order are 30.504, 29.104, 30.484, 32.095 and 31.656 MPa (−4.02 % to +5.85 % vs. reference; recovered nodal σxx at (0, 1, t/2) at h = 0.05 is 29.92 MPa against the Kirsch anchor of 30.0).

**Quarter-junction diagnosis and fix (M19, `f620a91`).** Prior to M19, the quarter model reported 45.48 MPa at h = 0.14 and 45.59 MPa at h = 0.07 (+50 % error that did not decay under refinement). Diagnostic instruments (`crates/fieldsmith-verify/examples/v3quarter.rs` and `v3half.rs`) isolated the cause at the mouth where the x = 0 symmetry plane meets the hole arc at (0, 1): on composite boolean fields (`max(plate, −hole)`), ` ±ulp` coordinate noise (5.6e−17 to 3.6e−15) on the zero patch caused a zero-threshold bisection to collapse onto the bracket end and drop the (0, 1) mouth apex node, while bisecting on `raw > tiny` pinned C2 zero-claims to the `tiny` band edge (`−8.28e−10` off the plane) and emitted planar slivers. Bisecting `positive_boundary` on `raw(p) > NOISE` (`NOISE = 1e-14`, 60 iterations, covered by `crates/fieldsmith-mesh/tests/mouth_junction.rs`) places the mouth apex cleanly at (0, 1, z) with zero off-plane pinned nodes.

With that fix, the quarter model's graze peaks drop from 45.48 / 45.59 MPa to 29.322 MPa (−3.30 % at h = 0.14, recovered nodal σxx = 28.43 MPa at (0, 1, 0.07)) and 32.399 MPa (+6.85 % at h = 0.07). Across the four spacings that share the same peak element centroid (h = 0.2, 0.1, 0.07, 0.05), the quarter model reads a uniform +0.93 % to +0.98 % higher than the half model (30.804, 30.767, 32.399, 31.956 MPa vs. 30.504, 30.484, 32.095, 31.656 MPa) because `ux = 0` on `x = 0` models symmetric uniform traction on both x = ±10, whereas the half model constrains the entire x = −10 end flat (`ux = 0`) and pulls x = +10.
